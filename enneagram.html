Enneagram for Leaders & Teams

Patterns become more useful when you can see them.

All of us develop habitual ways of paying attention. Things we notice first. Things we miss. Ways we respond when pressure rises. Strategies that once worked so well that we stopped noticing we were using them.

The Enneagram offers one way of making those patterns more visible. Not so that you can acquire another label. So that you have more choice.

How I use the Enneagram ↓

More Than A Type

The point is not to put you in a box.

Used badly, any personality framework can become another way of simplifying people. That is not how I use the Enneagram. For me, its value lies in helping us notice recurring patterns of attention, motivation and behaviour.

What do I instinctively notice?

What do I repeatedly miss?

What am I trying to make happen?

What am I trying to avoid?

What happens to me under pressure?

What becomes available when I stop responding automatically?

The type is not the destination. Greater awareness and greater choice are.

The map is useful only if it helps you move.

The Framework

A way of noticing what repeatedly pulls our attention.

The Enneagram describes nine broad patterns through which people tend to organise attention, motivation and behaviour. It goes beyond describing surface preferences. Used developmentally, it can help explore:

  • habitual attention
  • underlying motivations
  • recurring strengths
  • blind spots
  • responses to pressure
  • relational patterns
  • strategies for getting needs met
  • possibilities for greater flexibility

iEQ9

Assessment is the beginning of the conversation, not the answer.

Robert is certified to use the iEQ9 assessment with individuals and teams. The iEQ9 assessment provides a structured starting point for exploring likely Enneagram patterns. The report can offer useful hypotheses about attention, motivation, instinct, behaviour and development. But a report cannot tell you who you are. We use it as material for enquiry.

  • What resonates?
  • What does not?
  • What surprises you?
  • What have you perhaps normalised because it has always been there?

The report gives us somewhere to begin. Your lived experience matters more than the score.

For Leaders

Sometimes the pattern is working perfectly. It is simply no longer helping.

Recognition moments might include:

  • you keep responding to different situations in surprisingly similar ways
  • pressure exaggerates parts of your leadership
  • delegation is harder than it should be
  • conflict creates a predictable response
  • your strengths are beginning to have unintended consequences
  • you understand what you should do but still find yourself doing something else
  • a larger role is asking for greater range from you

The Enneagram can give us language for those patterns. The purpose is not to replace Executive Coaching. It can become one lens within the coaching when it is useful.

For Teams

Difference becomes easier to work with when we can talk about it.

Teams do not struggle simply because people are different. Difficulty often comes from the meaning we make of those differences. One person pushes. Another withdraws. Someone wants more information. Someone else wants a decision. Someone challenges. Someone keeps the peace.

The Enneagram can give a team a shared language for exploring some of those patterns without turning them into fixed identities. It can help a team notice:

  • different attentional habits
  • reactions under pressure
  • communication patterns
  • sources of misunderstanding
  • assumptions about one another
  • overused strengths
  • areas where the team may collectively have blind spots

A team is more than the sum of its individual profiles. The Enneagram can support team work. It is not a substitute for working with what is actually happening between people.

A Useful Lens, Not A Verdict

Your type is not your identity.

Not a box

A pattern describes something you do. It does not describe everything you are.

Not an excuse

Understanding why we behave a certain way does not remove responsibility for its impact.

Not a ranking

There is no best type.

Not the whole answer

The Enneagram is one developmental lens among several available in the work.

The purpose is not better labelling. It is greater freedom of response.

How It Works

Start with the question, not the tool.

We begin with what you are trying to understand or change. If the Enneagram looks like a useful lens, we can introduce it.

For an individual, this may involve:

Assessment

Complete the appropriate iEQ9 assessment.

Exploration

Use the report as the starting point for a deeper conversation.

Application

Connect what we notice to real leadership situations, relationships and choices.

Development

Experiment with increasing your range rather than simply understanding your pattern.

For teams, the process is shaped around the team's purpose and context rather than simply sharing everyone's type.

One Lens Among Several

Sometimes the Enneagram helps us see something. Sometimes another lens is more useful.

My work draws on Narrative Coaching, systemic thinking, embodiment, leadership development, team coaching and other approaches alongside the Enneagram. I am less interested in applying a favourite tool than in understanding what the situation actually needs.

Sometimes an Enneagram pattern makes something suddenly visible. Sometimes the more useful question is about story. Relationship. Authority. The system around you. Or simply what needs to happen next.
