Tending the Flame

How we stay alive to the work of change

Robert Stephenson · November 2026 · 5 minute read

Something catches your attention. A question stays with you. A conversation opens a possibility you hadn’t considered. For a moment, you can see a different way forward.

Then that possibility has to find a place in your life.

There are people who need you, commitments you have made and a diary that was already full before this new idea arrived. Where does it go? What helps you return to it when the moment that sparked it has passed?

That question was at the heart of Tending the Flame: How We Stay Alive to the Work of Change, my talk at Optimus Coach Academy’s Igniting Change conference.

It is a question for anyone who leads, coaches or helps others through change. We can spend so much time attending to what others need that our own curiosity, energy and sense of possibility receive whatever attention is left.

What would it mean to tend those things deliberately?

Which spark is yours?

After a day full of ideas, it is tempting to bring everything home. A different way of working. A new direction. Perhaps a completely reinvented self, ready for Monday morning.

I’m interested in the quieter question: which spark keeps drawing you back?

There may be a difference between the idea you think you should choose and the one you want to explore. Even that want may have layers. As you give it attention, you may discover another desire beneath the first: something less familiar, harder to admit or more personally meaningful.

You can give it room without making a final commitment. Walk a little way along its path. Notice what becomes visible from there. Come back and look again.

Three questions run through this exploration:

  • What story am I living?
  • What parts of myself have I left outside that story?
  • What might become possible if I allowed more of myself to belong?

The answer may involve what you do next. It may also involve who you allow yourself to be as you do it.

Giving it room to live

In the talk, I offered FLAMES as scaffolding for reflection: Focus, Love, Align, Meaning, Emerge and Sustain.

I hold these as invitations. A phrase from Bronwyn Nash, a trainer I have worked with, stays with me here: “A playground, not a prison.” A framework can give us somewhere to explore while leaving room for discovery.

Focus invites us to give one possibility our attention. The choice can be provisional. We can discover more by staying with one question than by trying to pursue every idea at once.

Love asks what we care enough about to nurture. What might we say no to so that this yes has room? And have we included ourselves in that care? Rest, nourishment, space between commitments and someone to think with can be part of tending the work.

Align brings us into a conversation with what matters to us, what we believe and what we can bring. It also asks what we have left waiting in the wings. Perhaps playfulness has had little room in our professional identity. Perhaps warmth, courage or uncertainty has been waiting for an invitation. What small adjustment would let more of us participate?

Meaning gives us time to find our own language. An idea may matter deeply to the person who shared it. What does it mean in our life, our team or our wider world? Sometimes we discover that meaning as we hear ourselves speaking to someone who is listening with curiosity.

Emerge leaves space for what is still taking shape. A feeling, an image or a question may arrive before a clear explanation. We can listen, remain curious and allow time before deciding what it means. Humour and playfulness can help us loosen an answer we have been gripping too tightly.

Sustain asks how we will return. What is the next small step? What care do we need? When will we come back to see what happened and choose again?

You might need one of these invitations more than the others today. You might return to the same one several times.

Who will walk with you?

A possibility can change when we share it with someone who gives it attention. They may hear something we have overlooked, ask a useful question or remember what mattered when we have become distracted.

Who could offer that to you? And who might welcome it from you?

In a team, this might mean making room to revisit an idea after the meeting has ended. In our own work, it might mean asking someone to walk alongside us while we try something unfamiliar.

For now, choose one spark and finish three sentences:

My next small step is…

The care I need is…

I will return to this…

Then consider who you want to tell.

Take your spark with you. Tend it, tend yourself and keep turning towards each other.

We keep the fire alive together.
